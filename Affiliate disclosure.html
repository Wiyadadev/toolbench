Toolbench is reader-supported. This page explains, plainly, how that works.

How we make money

Some of the tools we write about pay us a commission if you click through from this site and later become a paying customer. This is called affiliate marketing. It does not cost you anything extra — the price you pay is the same whether you use our link or go directly to the company.

Does this affect what we recommend?

Our policy is to only feature tools we've actually used for a real task, not tools chosen because they pay the highest commission. A tool being listed on this site does not mean every mention includes an affiliate link, and an affiliate link does not mean the tool received a better review than it earned. Where we have a paid or free trial relationship with a vendor, or where a link is an affiliate link, we aim to say so at the point it appears.

What this looks like in practice

  • Buttons and links marked as leading to a partner tool may be affiliate links once our applications with each program are approved.
  • We do not accept payment in exchange for a positive review.
  • We do not alter a tool's rating or verdict based on commission rate.
  • If a tool changes for the worse after we've reviewed it, we aim to update the review rather than leave outdated praise up to protect a commission.

Why we're telling you this

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's Endorsement Guides require this kind of disclosure, and — separately from the legal requirement — we think you're owed a clear answer to "how does this site make money" before you act on anything we recommend.
